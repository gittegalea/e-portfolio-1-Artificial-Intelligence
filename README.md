## Artefact 2: A News article I found this week

<img width="949" height="629" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/d1db98a4-ffa4-4dad-9240-23ea21751774" />

https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-04-27/ai-helps-parks-victoria-manage-native-species-pests-after-fires/106589360

### Summary of the artefact
This short news article by Brown (2026) explains how Victorian rangers can use AI to protect native animals. The rangers collect lots of images with field cameras but it takes weeks for humans to check those images one by one. An AI tool developed by researchers and scientists uses high-speed AI to process 20 images per second and can identify animals at greater than 95% accuracy. The technology was specifically trained on Austrailian fauna. 5 millions images were used to train the system. It save both time and money. Moreover, it means scientist can focus on interpreting results quicker and making decisions about native animals sooner. For example, feral pigs are a huge threat to our native animals and authorities must control the situation. The AI recognition model is available to the community as an open source program.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact
I chose this article because it was about the positive use of AI. I find all the negative reports of AI depressing. The use of AI in image processing and doing work faster and better than humans is exciting, and using technology to help us better protect our native animals and threatened species is important for the environment and our society. The article was easy to read and had lots of images and a video so it was visually stimulating. I am excited that the technology is open source and they have taken data security and intellectual property rights very seriously. I also like that the article had a interactive component, I had to slide a frame over an image to see the identified animal, showing how the AI tool can spot animals that a human would have most likely missed.

## Artefact 3: Scholary Article

<img width="509" height="581" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/bb1c1292-40e6-4a93-ae3d-19d4527ea1c7" />

### Summary of the aretfact
The article by Chinta et al. (2025) investigates how AI can enhancing efficiency and effectiveness in healthcare in such areas as cardiology, dermatology and emergency medicine. The AI tools improve diagnosing patients with better accuracy, personalising treatments and predicting patient outcomes based on probabilities from machine learning. The article also discusses concerns over ethical and fairness challenges related to biases in the data and algorithms. For example, 'an algorithm used in US hospitals was biased against black patients in resource allocation' (Chinta et al. 2025, p 2)

### Justification on why I chose the artefact
I chose this article because I am interested in and concerned about how AI is used in healthcare. I found this article on Google Scholar by searching "AI in healthcare" and choosing the article from 2025, as it was more recent. I read the abstract and the conclusion and  I found these two sections to be informative. However, I did not understand what was meant by bias, I needed examples so I went looking further in the article. I was shocked to learn 'AI tools for diagnosing depression have faced challenges when applied across different linguistic and cultural backgrounds because they were primarily trained on English-speaking, Western populations, leading to potential misdiagnoses in non-Western patients' (Chinta et al. 2025, p 2). This has significant consequences for people from different cultures. I noticed that the article was written by academics in the United States, so this has a US focus. But living in Australia it would concern me if these AI algortims were being used here.
